2. The 8 lines everyone must remember

  1. Our AI is a decision tree. It is rules, not ChatGPT, not a neural network.
  2. It looks at heart rate, head movement, and how long it lasts.
  3. Heart rate is half the score because Polar H10 is a chest strap. That number is the most trustworthy.
  4. Movement is 30% because a scared person fidgets, but looking around also moves the head, so it is not the main vote.
  5. Time is 20% so a 1-second jump is not treated as a panic attack.
  6. 10 means about +20 BPM over your own rest. 18 means about +36 BPM.
  7. We compare to your 60-second baseline, not a random 72 BPM from a textbook.
  8. Pressing A or X is a human exit. That is not the AI.

3. How we made the formula (say this)

Do not say “it was in the SRS” and freeze. Do not say ChatGPT. Say we designed a 3-part score.

“We asked: what can we actually measure in VR? Polar gives heart rate. The headset gives head movement. We can count how many seconds it stays high. A panic feeling is not one spike. It is the heart going up, the body getting restless, and it not going away. So we made one score from those three things.”

Then the weights, in plain words:

“We gave heart rate 50% because Polar is ECG on the chest. That is the real signal. We gave movement 30% as backup. We gave time 20% so a short jump does not shut the session. The three parts add to 100%.”

Then the 10 and 18:

“If the person is sitting still, the score is basically half of the BPM rise. So if your rest is 75 and you go to 95, that is +20, score is 10: getting stressed, make it easier. If you jump about +36 BPM, score is 18: too much, Safe Room. We picked 10 and 18 so those two heart-rate jumps are the two lines.”

6. Tiny Q and A

This is not AI.

It is a decision tree. Old-school AI. If this, then that. It reads sensors and then it changes the session. That is still AI. We never said it was ChatGPT.

Show the model file.

There is no file. The rules are in the code. We can walk them.

Why not machine learning?

That needs hundreds of labelled panic sessions. We do not have that. A guessy model that sometimes sends you to Safe Room is worse. Rules we can explain are safer.

Why 95 and 108?

In the headset Polar rest is often 70 to 95, not 60. So 95 can still be Advanced. 108 is a bit high, so we start easier. Higher than that is Beginner.

Is stress real?

Stress on the graph is our score. The BPM under it is real Polar. If you stand up, BPM goes up, score goes up. That is what we mean by stress here.

What if someone is an athlete?

We subtract their own rest. 55 to 75 is the same kind of jump as 80 to 100.

Appointments?

Out of scope.

Live Polar on the Quest alone, no laptop?

No. Laptop reads the strap and sends BPM to Unity. Demo is Quest Link.
