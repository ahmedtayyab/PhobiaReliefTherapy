3. 60-second opening

“This is a VR exposure therapy app. The patient logs in, we take a 60-second baseline of Polar H10 heart rate and headset movement, then a decision-tree AI picks Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced. 6. Why we chose those values

Three buckets. If you mix them, they will think you made the SRS up after coding.

From the SRS (we implemented the spec)

  • Formula 0.5 / 0.3 / 0.2
  • Classes Calm / Panic / Severe at 10 and 18
  • Actions continue / repeat level / Safe Room
  • 1-second sampling, 15-second time window
  • AI named as Decision Tree or Random Forest - we took the tree

From physiology plus Polar in a headset

  • Adult rest is roughly 60-100 BPM. Our rest band is 55-95 because seated Polar in VR sits high, often 70-95, not a lying-down lab 60.
  • Stage 3 ceiling 95 = top of that rest band, so a calm patient is not punished into Beginner.
  • Stage 2 ceiling 108 = mild tachycardia. Higher rest means start easy.
  • With movement zero, PanicScore = 0.5 x dHR, so the SRS integers 10 and 18 are a +20 BPM rise and a +36 BPM rise. +20 over personal rest is a real sympathetic bump while seated. +36 on a 75 rest is 111 BPM, tachycardia.
  • Heart rate gets 50% because Polar H10 is chest ECG. Head movement is 30% because looking around a 360 photo is noisy. Time is 20% so a 1-second spike is not a panic attack.

Safety we added after live tests

  • SRS said immediate evaluate around dHR 25-28. Live Polar fidgeted into that too easily, so code uses 32 and 36.
  • Abort needs score 22 and dHR 20 and 20 s grace, not every 18+.
  • We lower difficulty in-session. We do not raise it in-session. VRET should not spike while they are inside the stimulus.
Short version: “SRS gave the formula and the three classes. Polar in VR set the rest gates at 95 and 108. Safety tests set 32, 36, and the 20-second grace. None of this is a trained neural net.”

7. Questioning: AI and thresholds

This is the main block. Answer in two or three sentences. Offer the file name if they want to see code: PanicDetectionService.cs and ExposureStageAdvisor.cs.

This is not AI. It is just if-else.

A decision tree at runtime is nested if-else. ID3 and CART produce the same shape. Ours is a knowledge-based tree: features from Polar and the headset, classes from the SRS, actions that change the session. Russell and Norvig define AI as agents that perceive and act. ISO 22989 is recommendations for a human-defined goal. Machine learning is one subfield, not the definition. Our SRS literally says AI (Decision Tree / Random Forest).

Where is the model file?

There is none. The model is the code. Same inputs always give the same stage and the same recommendation. For a safety loop that is a strength.

How can you call a formula AI?

The formula is the scoring function. The AI is score plus tree plus action. Hospitals do the same with early-warning scores: weighted vitals, then a protocol. 0.5 dHR + 0.3 movement + 0.2 time is that score.

Where did 0.5, 0.3, 0.2 come from?

SRS section 3.2.5 and UC-10. We did not fit them with gradient descent. Heart rate is half because Polar H10 is the clinical-grade channel. Movement supports it. Time stops a blink of tachycardia counting as panic. They sum to 1 so they are percentages of influence.

How did you choose 10 and 18?

They are the SRS classification bands. They also match physiology: if the patient is still, score is 0.5 times the rise in BPM, so 10 means +20 over their own baseline and 18 means +36. We did not pick 10 and 18 as round exam numbers after the fact. The spec and the heart-rate math agree.

How did you choose 95 and 108 for starting stage?

95 is the top of our resting band 55-95. Live Polar rest in the headset is usually 70-95, so a calm person can still get Advanced. 108 is mild tachycardia. Above that, Tree A starts them at Beginner. Movement 16 and 28 is the second test on the same tree.

Are these thresholds accurate?

Polar BPM is accurate as a chest ECG strap. The classes are a transparent heuristic, not a DSM-5 diagnosis and not an F1 score. We have no labelled panic corpus. What we can claim: personal baseline, reproducible rules, and a safety bias (false lower-stage is better than missed distress). Tools, PRT, Run SRS Logic Tests checks the formula without a headset.

Is the stress even real?

On the graphs, stress is PanicScore, a proxy for sympathetic arousal relative to that session’s baseline. It is believable because the input is live Polar, because it is dHR not raw BPM, and because you can watch it rise if the wearer stands or breathes fast. It is not cortisol and not a psychologist’s SUDS rating. We say proxy, not diagnosis.

Why not Random Forest or a neural net?

A forest needs labelled rows of (dHR, HM, TS, what a clinician said). We do not have that. Training on ten classmates would overfit and would be a worse thing to put on a Safe Room abort. The SRS allowed Decision Tree or Random Forest. We shipped the tree so we can walk every branch. Future work is log SUDS and compare a trained model against this expert tree, keeping this tree as fallback.

Why is heart rate weighted more than movement?

Polar H10 is ECG. Headset motion in a 360 photo includes ordinary looking around. If we weighted movement first, exploring the scene would look like panic. Time spent only accumulates when dHR is above 16 or movement is 2.4 times baseline, so a glance does not fill the 20% term either.

Walk through a number.

Baseline 75, now 95, sitting still, no stress seconds: dHR = 20, score = 10, class = Panic Detected, AI says Repeat Level, after 8 seconds we drop one stage. Same person at 111 BPM: dHR = 36, score forced to 22, Severe, Safe Room if 20 seconds have passed. That is the board example. Use it.

What can you change later?

Weights, 10 and 18, 95 and 108, 32 and 36, the 20-second grace, the 8-second cooldown. Those are constants. We would change them with labelled sessions, not by gut on demo day. We would not turn manual Beginner buttons back on for the default patient path.

Why 60 seconds baseline, not 8 minutes?

An early requirements paragraph said 8 minutes. The use case we implemented is 1 minute before each 2-minute exposure, so the baseline matches this session’s rest, including today’s caffeine or nerves. Long clinical intake is out of scope.

Why can it lower difficulty but not raise it during the 2 minutes?

Safety. If they are coping, the HUD may recommend a higher stage after this session. Jumping Advanced while they are still on the balcony would violate graded exposure.

What if they freeze instead of fidgeting?

We track freeze. It is not in the panic formula. That is a real gap. A still, terrified patient is scored mainly from heart rate, and they still have A/X. We do not pretend freeze is solved.

Caffeine, athletes, beta blockers?

Baseline eats resting differences. We classify change, not 72 vs 90. Drugs that flatten HR make the tree conservative. Emergency still works.

Is this a medical device?

No. Student VRET prototype with a documented decision policy. Not FDA, not a diagnosis.

8. If they wander off AI

Keep answers short and steer back. They said the focus would be AI; do not volunteer a 10-minute XR lecture.

Why 360 photos, not a 3D city?

One skybox, stable 90 Hz, small build. Intensity is exposure on the material, and darkness has three images by stage. The AI still has a real stimulus.

Why world-space UI?

Screen-space overlay is invisible in the headset. We convert to world space, scale about 0.001, XRI laser. Grip drags the menu.

How does Polar reach Unity?

Python bleak on the PC reads BLE, UDP 7878 to Unity. Quest Link. Not a Quest-only APK path.

Where is data stored?

JSON locally, Supabase when online. Offline session still saves.

Appointments?

Out of scope. Do not apologize at length.

Why Unity coroutines?

Unity is one game thread. Sleep would freeze the headset. Timers yield.

Show tests.

Tools -> PRT -> Run SRS Logic Tests. Calm < 10, large dHR can force Safe Room, elevated baseline cannot start Advanced.

9. What we admit (this helps)

  • Not a trained Random Forest.
  • Not diagnostic accuracy, no ROC curve.
  • Appointments not built.
  • Live Polar needs the PC bridge.
  • Freeze is tracked and unused in the score.
  • Old write-ups that say 20 s / 30 s are stale. Code is 60 / 120.
Never: machine learning, trained model, 90% accurate, “the AI thinks,” live Polar on a standalone APK, appointments are coming next week.
