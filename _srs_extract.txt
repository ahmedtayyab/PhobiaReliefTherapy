Abstract
Phobia Relief Therapy is a VR based therapeutic system developed to help individuals gradually overcome common phobias such as fear of heights, water, or social situations. These fears can significantly limit daily activities and quality of life. Traditional exposure therapy conducted by psychologists can be expensive, difficult to access, and potentially unsafe when real world exposure is involved. The proposed system addresses this problem by providing a safe and immersive self guided therapy environment using Virtual Reality technology. Users can log in, select a specific phobia to address, and begin a controlled VR exposure session. During the session the system continuously collects physiological and behavioral data through wearable gadgets such as a Polar H10 heart rate sensor and motion tracking from the VR headset to monitor the user's stress levels. This data is analyzed in real time to detect signs of panic. The system can then adapt the exposure intensity or trigger safety mechanisms to ensure psychological safety. After the session the system provides feedback on the user's performance and progress. The system also maintains session records for users and administrators. An administrator panel allows for user management and therapy performance analysis. Phobia Relief Therapy thus creates a unified digital system connecting users with an adaptive and accessible mental health tool while maintaining data security and user privacy. This Software Requirements Specification outlines the system's functional, non functional, and design requirements forming the foundation for its development and evaluation.

1. Introduction
This document presents the Software Requirement Specification (SRS) for the project Phobia Relief Therapy. The system is designed to address challenges in mental healthcare specifically in the treatment of common phobias using exposure therapy. It aims to provide a safe, accessible, and adaptive VR based therapeutic environment coupled with physiological monitoring to enhance the treatment process.
The SRS defines the problem, objectives, scope, stakeholders, functional and non functional requirements and provides a clear foundation for the development of the proposed solution.
2. Vision Document
 Problem Statement
Table 1: Problem Statement
Category
Description
Problem
Individuals suffering from phobias such as fear of heights, water, or social situations often avoid confronting their fears, which reinforces the phobia over time.
Traditional exposure therapy conducted by psychologists is expensive and requires multiple in person sessions.
Access to qualified mental health professionals is limited, especially in rural or underserved areas, leading to long waiting lists.
Real world exposure therapy is logistically difficult to arrange, cannot be precisely controlled, and may pose actual physical or psychological risks to the patient.
Affects
  .      Individuals with specific phobias who cannot afford or access traditional therapy.
The mental well being and daily functioning of those with untreated phobias.
Impact
  Increased anxiety and reduced quality of life due to unaddressed fears.
Delayed treatment and worsening of phobia symptoms over time.
   Reliance on medication as a substitute for effective exposure based therapy.
Solution
• A VR Based Phobia Relief Therapy System that provides safe and controlled exposure to fear inducing stimuli.• Users can access therapy from home using a VR headset without needing to visit a clinic.• The system creates immersive virtual environments tailored to specific phobias such as heights, water, or crowd anxiety.• Real time physiological monitoring through heart rate sensors detects stress and panic levels during sessions.• Automatic safety mechanisms such as pausing the session or teleporting the user to a safe room prevent overwhelming distress.• Session feedback and progress tracking provide users with measurable outcomes and motivation for continued use.
 Business Opportunities
The Phobia Relief Therapy project offers several practical business opportunities. The software can be sold directly to consumers as a one time purchase or subscription for individuals who already own VR headsets. It can also be licensed to therapy clinics and mental health professionals as a tool to use with patients. Partnerships with VR arcades and wellness centers provide another revenue stream. Academic institutions conducting research on exposure therapy may license the system for studies. The adaptive framework can later be expanded to address related conditions such as social anxiety or public speaking fear, creating additional products from the same technical foundation.
 Objectives
The major objectives of Phobia Relief Therapy are:
To provide a safe and accessible platform for individuals to confront and manage their phobias from home.
To reduce the cost and logistical barriers associated with traditional exposure therapy.
To create immersive and realistic VR environments tailored to specific phobias including heights, water, and social situations.
To utilize real time physiological data to create an adaptive and safe therapeutic experience preventing overwhelming panic.
To provide users with clear feedback on their progress during and after each session.
To offer administrators a tool to manage users and monitor system usage.
 Scope
A VR based application accessible to users with compatible VR headsets and a companion web interface for account management and progress tracking.
User features: Secure login, phobia selection, VR exposure sessions, real time physiological monitoring, adaptive intensity control, safety mechanisms, session feedback, session history, and progress tracking.
Administrator features: System monitoring, user account management, and viewing of anonymized usage analytics.
The system ensures secure data handling and privacy of user information in compliance with data protection standards.
 Constraints
High cost of VR headsets may limit accessibility for some users.
Accuracy of panic detection depends on the quality of sensor data and the reliability of detection algorithms.
Users may experience motion sickness or discomfort in VR, requiring clear onboarding and safety guidelines.
Development time is limited to the academic semester, requiring prioritization of core features.

3. System Requirements Specification
3.1 System Features
The Phobia Relief Therapy system offers a range of essential features to improve mental healthcare accessibility and user experience:
User Access Panel: Secure registration, login, and role-based dashboards (users and admin).
Therapy Session Selection: Users can browse and select phobia specific therapy modules such as fear of heights, water, or social situations.
VR Environment Loading: The system loads immersive 3D environments corresponding to the selected phobia and difficulty level.
Physiological Monitoring: Real time collection of heart rate data from wearable sensors such as the Polar H10.
Adaptive Exposure Control: The system adjusts environment intensity based on the user's physiological responses to maintain an optimal therapeutic zone.
Panic Detection and Safety Mechanisms: Automatic detection of distress triggers safety responses including intensity reduction, session pause, or teleportation to a safe room.
Admin Panel: Tools for managing user accounts, viewing usage analytics, and managing VR content.
Session Feedback and Progress Tracking: Session summaries showing duration, heart rate data, panic events, and progress over time.
3.2 Functional Requirements
The system must fulfill the following functional requirements:
3.2.1 User Authentication and Registration
The system allows users and administrators to register by providing required details such as name, email, and password.
The system shall verify new users via email verification before account activation.
The system shall allow registered users to log in using secure credentials.
The system shall provide role based dashboards after successful login, including User Dashboard and Admin Dashboard.
The system shall allow users to reset their password via a secure forgot password flow.
3.2.2 Phobia and Therapy Session Selection
The system shall allow users to browse available phobia therapy modules such as fear of heights, fear of water, and fear of public speaking.
The system shall display information about each therapy module including a description and recommended starting level.
The system shall allow users to select a specific session and difficulty level such as Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced.
The system shall confirm the user's readiness and provide pre-session instructions including ensuring the heart rate monitor is connected.
3.2.3 VR Environment Loading and Management
The system shall load the correct 3D VR environment corresponding to the user's phobia and difficulty selection.
The system shall establish a connection with the VR headset and physiological sensors before the session begins.
The system should provide a virtual Safe Room where users can prepare for and recover from sessions.
The system shall display a countdown or ready prompt before transporting the user to the main exposure environment.
3.2.4 Physiological and Behavioral Data Collection
The system shall collect 8 minutes of baseline data before the first exposure session to establish user's normal heart rate and movement patterns.
The system shall continuously collect heart rate data from the Polar H10 sensor via Bluetooth throughout the session.
The system shall continuously collect positional and rotational data from the VR headset to monitor:  Head movement speed ( rotation speed )
Head movement travel ( distance covered )
Freezing behaviour ( near-zero velocity ) 
Jerky/erratic movements
3.2.5 Real Time Panic Detection
The system shall calculate ΔHR (Delta Heart Rate) as:ΔHR = Current Heart Rate - Baseline Heart Rate
The system shall monitor Head Movement (HM) using two metrics:
-Rotation speed
- Movement travel
The system shall track Time Spent (TS) in high-stress states, with panic time < 15 seconds indicating acute stress.
The system shall calculate a Panic Score using the weighted formula:Panic Score = (ΔHR × 0.5) + (HM × 0.3) + (TS × 0.2)
The system shall classify panic based on the following thresholds:
Panic Score
Classification
System Action
0-10
Calm Stress
Continuous Exposure at low level
10-18
Panic Detected
Repeat Current Difficulty Level
18+
Severe Panic
Stop Session / Teleport to safe room
If ΔHR > 28, the system shall immediately evaluate the Panic Score as panic risk is high.
The system shall log all detected panic events with timestamps, ΔHR, HM, TS, and calculated Panic Score.
3.2.6 Safety Mechanisms
Upon detection of a panic state the system shall automatically trigger an appropriate safety mechanism.
The user shall have a manual Emergency Stop button accessible at all times within the VR environment.
The system shall log all safety mechanism triggers with timestamps and the associated physiological data.
3.2.7 Adaptive Exposure Intensity Control
The system shall continuously monitor user data to determine the appropriate intensity level during the session.
If the user's stress levels are low and stable ,the system recommends advancing to next level.
If stress levels rise too quickly the system shall decrease the intensity and put users back to safe environment.
3.2.8 Session Feedback
After a session ends whether completed, paused, or aborted the system shall generate a feedback summary
The summary shall include session duration, maximum heart rate, average heart rate, number of panic events, and number of safety mechanism triggers.
The system shall store all feedback data in the user's history for long term tracking.
3.2.9 Session History and Progress Tracking
The system shall allow users to view a list of all their past therapy sessions.
Users shall be able to select a past session to view the detailed feedback report generated for that session.
The system shall display progress over time such as trends in session duration, peak heart rate, or panic event frequency
3.2.10 User Dashboard
The system should provide a personalized dashboard for each user after logging in.
The dashboard shall show quick access to start new session, view progress and profile.
The dashboard shall display a brief summary of the user’s latest progress or recent session activity.
3.2.11 Manage User Profile
The system shall allow users to view and edit their profile information including name, email, and password.
3.2.12 Notifications and Alerts
The system shall provide in app notifications for account verification, password resets, and optionally session reminders.
The system may provide email notifications for the same events based on user preferences.
3.2.13 Manage Appointments
Lab staff shall be able to view, confirm, reschedule, or cancel patient appointments.
The system shall allow phlebotomist assignment for each booking.
Changes in appointment status shall automatically update the patient’s dashboard.
3.2.14 Administrator Dashboard
The system shall provide a dedicated dashboard for the Administrator upon login.
The dashboard shall display key system metrics such as total registered users, total sessions completed, and average session duration.
The admin shall be able to view user details, suspend or reactivate accounts, and reset user passwords if necessary.
Non-Functional Requirements
The non-functional requirements ensure quality, usability, and       security of system.
3.3.1 User-Friendly Interface
The web dashboard must feature a simple and intuitive interface suitable for users with varying levels of technical comfort.
The VR interface must be simple with clear visual cues and easily accessible safety controls.
3.3.2 Data Security
All data must be accessible only by authorized users being the user themselves and the administrator for support purposes.
3.3.3 Performance
The VR application must maintain a consistent frame rate of at least 90 frames per second to prevent motion sickness and ensure immersion.
The system must handle real time data streaming from sensors with latency under 100 milliseconds to enable effective panic detection.
The web dashboard and session history pages must load within 3 to 5 seconds under normal load conditions.
3.3.4 Privacy
The system must ensure confidentiality of user data.
Anonymized data may be used for aggregate analytics but individual user data must never be exposed to other users.
Users must provide explicit consent for data collection and be informed about how their data will be used.
3.3.5 Compatibility
The  VR application must be compatible with at least one major VR headset platform such as Oculus Quest or HTC Vive.
The system must integrate with specific supported physiological sensors like polar H10

Use Case 10: Panic Detection
Table 29: Panic Detection
Use case ID:
UC-10
Use case Name:
Track Reports
Created by:
Sehar Saleem
Last updated by:
Shehroz Abbas
Date Created:
03/15/2026
Revision Date:
03/16/2026
Actor:
System
Description:
System calculates ΔHR, Head Movement score, and Time Spent to generate Panic Score. Uses AI (Decision Tree/Random Forest) to classify panic level and recommend action.
Trigger:
Start Exposure begins (UC-09).
Pre-condition:
Baseline data exists. Current HR and movement data are being received from UC-08.
Post- condition:
Panic event is logged if detected. AI recommendation is generated. Safety action may be triggered.
Normal flow:
System receives current data every second from UC-08:- Heart rate- Rotation speed- Movement frequency
System calculates ΔHR = Current HR - Baseline HR.
 System calculates Head Movement (HM) Score:- Combines rotation speed and movement frequency- Scaled to 0-100 based on deviation from baseline
 System calculates Time Spent (TS) Score:- Tracks consecutive seconds where HR or movement exceeds thresholds- If panic time < 15 seconds → high stress- Scaled to 0-100
 System computes Panic Score = (ΔHR × 0.5) + (HM × 0.3) + (TS × 0.2).
 If ΔHR > 25, system flags as high priority.
 System sends Panic Score to AI model (Decision Tree/Random Forest).
 AI model classifies panic level:- 0 - 10: Calm stress → AI recommends "Continue Exposure"- 10 - 18: Panic detected → AI recommends "Repeat Level"- 18+: Severe panic → AI recommends "Stop / Safe Room"
 System logs panic event with:- Timestamp- ΔHR value- Head Movement Score- Time Spent Score- Calculated Panic Score- AI classification- AI recommendation
If score ≥ 18, system triggers safety mechanism.
System continues monitoring until session ends.
Alternative flow:
 10a. Safety mechanism triggered.   10a.1. System pauses exposure.   10a.2. System teleports user to safe room.   10a.3. Session ends.
Exceptions:
Database error. Permission denied.
Includes:
N/A
Frequency of use:
Every second during every session.
Special Requirements:
Low latency processing. AI model trained on panic data.
